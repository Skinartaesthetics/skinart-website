How to Start Using Retinol: A Beginner’s Guide for Winter Skin

At-Home Skincare November 2026 By Dina Telesh

Retinol is one of the most talked-about ingredients in skincare, and for good reason. It is also one of the most misunderstood. Many clients tell me they tried retinol once, their skin turned red and flaky, and they gave up. Almost every time, the problem was not the ingredient. It was how they started. If you have been wondering how to start using retinol, especially now that cold weather and indoor heat are already testing your skin, here is a calm, step-by-step approach.

What retinol is (and what it is not)

Retinol is a form of vitamin A. It belongs to a larger family called retinoids, which also includes prescription-strength options such as tretinoin. Over-the-counter retinol is gentler because the skin has to convert it into its active form, which is also why results take patience.

Used consistently, retinol can help skin look smoother, more even, and more refined. It supports cell turnover, so it is often part of routines focused on fine lines, uneven texture, dullness, and clogged pores. What retinol is not is a quick fix. Most people need several weeks of steady use before they notice a difference, and it works best as part of a balanced routine rather than on its own.

How to start using retinol, step by step

1. Begin low and slow

Choose a lower-strength product designed for beginners and apply it just two or three nights a week. If your skin stays comfortable for a couple of weeks, you can slowly increase to every other night, and later nightly if your skin is happy. There is no prize for getting there quickly.

2. Use a pea-sized amount on dry skin

A pea-sized amount is enough for the whole face. Apply it to clean, completely dry skin, avoiding the corners of the nose, the lips, and the delicate skin right around the eyes unless the product is made for that area.

3. Try the “sandwich” method if you are sensitive

If your skin tends to react, apply a thin layer of moisturizer first, then your retinol, then another layer of moisturizer. This buffers the retinol so it feels gentler while still letting it work. It is an especially helpful approach for retinol in winter, when the skin barrier is already under stress.

4. Keep the rest of your routine simple

On retinol nights, skip scrubs, strong exfoliating acids, and other potent actives. A gentle cleanser, your retinol, and a nourishing moisturizer are all you need. You can still enjoy other ingredients, just not all on the same night.

5. Wear sunscreen every morning

Retinol can make skin more sensitive to the sun, and daily sun exposure undoes much of the good work you are doing. A broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning, even on gray Pennsylvania days, is non-negotiable.

Retinol in winter: adjusting for cold, dry air

Winter is actually a wonderful time to begin retinol, since the sun is less intense and many of us are indoors more. The trade-off is dryness. Cold outdoor air and forced-air heating already pull moisture from the skin, so retinol can feel stronger in December than it would in May.

To keep things comfortable, lean on a richer night cream, add a hydrating serum underneath your moisturizer, and do not be afraid to drop back to fewer nights a week during a cold snap. For a full seasonal routine, see our guide to protecting your skin from cold weather and dry indoor heat, and if you are not sure whether you are dealing with dryness or dehydration, read dehydrated skin vs. dry skin.

Retinol purging vs. irritation: how to tell the difference

Some people notice a few extra breakouts in the first weeks of using retinol. This is often called retinol purging: faster cell turnover can bring congestion that was already forming to the surface sooner. Purging typically appears in areas where you usually break out and tends to settle as your skin adjusts.

Irritation is different. Burning, stinging, widespread redness, raw-feeling patches, or flaking that keeps getting worse are signs your skin needs a break. Stop using retinol, focus on gentle cleansing and moisturizing, and restart more slowly once your skin feels calm. If breakouts appear in new areas or do not settle, check in with a professional.

Retinol and professional treatments

Retinol and professional treatments can work beautifully together, but timing matters. Many treatments, including chemical peels, microneedling, dermaplaning, and waxing, ask that you pause retinol for several days before and after your appointment. Always tell your esthetician what you are using at home, and follow the specific instructions you are given. When to check with a dermatologist or your doctor

Retinol is a cosmetic ingredient, but some situations call for medical guidance. If you are pregnant, trying to become pregnant, or breastfeeding, talk to your doctor before using any retinoid. If you have eczema, rosacea, or very reactive skin, or if you are considering prescription-strength retinoids for acne or other concerns, a dermatologist is the right person to guide you. And if irritation does not improve after you stop using a product, please have it looked at.

How to start using retinol: the takeaway

Starting retinol does not have to mean weeks of peeling and frustration. Begin with a gentle formula a few nights a week, use a small amount, buffer it with moisturizer if you need to, protect your skin with daily sunscreen, and give it time. Frequently Asked Questions

  • How often should I use retinol as a beginner?

    Start two or three nights a week with a lower-strength product. If your skin stays comfortable for a couple of weeks, slowly increase to every other night, and later nightly if your skin tolerates it.

  • Is it okay to start retinol in winter?

    Yes, many people find winter a good time to start because sun exposure is lower. Cold air and indoor heat can make skin drier, so pair retinol with a richer moisturizer and use it less often during cold snaps.

  • What is retinol purging, and how long does it last?

    Purging is a temporary increase in breakouts, usually in areas where you already tend to break out, as faster cell turnover brings existing congestion to the surface. It often settles within several weeks. Burning, stinging, or spreading redness is irritation, not purging.

  • What is the retinol sandwich method?

    Apply a thin layer of moisturizer, then your retinol, then another layer of moisturizer. The moisturizer buffers the retinol so it feels gentler, which is helpful for sensitive or dry skin.

  • Should I stop retinol before a facial or chemical peel?

    Often, yes. Many treatments, including chemical peels, microneedling, dermaplaning, and waxing, ask that you pause retinol for several days before and after. Tell your esthetician what you use at home and follow their specific instructions.

  • Who should talk to a doctor before using retinol?

    Anyone who is pregnant, trying to become pregnant, or breastfeeding should check with their doctor before using any retinoid. People with eczema, rosacea, or very reactive skin, or anyone considering prescription retinoids, should consult a dermatologist.
