What to Expect After a Chemical Peel — Day by Day

Post Care May 2026 By Dina Telesh

A professional chemical peel is one of the most effective treatments in skincare — and one of the most mismanaged at home. In my years performing peels at SkinArt Aesthetics in Huntingdon Valley, PA, the most common thing I see clients do wrong is go home and keep using their regular skincare routine as if nothing happened. The results suffer as a consequence. How you care for your skin in the days after your peel determines a significant part of your final outcome. This is a realistic, day-by-day guide to what to expect and exactly what to do.

First: your aftercare depends on which peel you had

Generic aftercare guides rarely address this, but your recovery looks different depending on which peel was used. At SkinArt, we work with four professional systems — and each has its own healing profile.

BioRePeel is our most popular no-downtime option. It is biostimulating rather than traditionally exfoliating, which means most clients experience little to no visible peeling — just some mild dryness or tightness in the first few days. You can typically return to your normal routine within five to seven days, though SPF remains non-negotiable throughout.

Face Reality peels are formulated specifically for acne-prone skin and tend to produce more visible peeling, particularly around the chin and jawline where congestion concentrates. It is common to experience some purging in the week following a Face Reality peel — breakouts appearing as the peel clears deeper congestion. This is normal and not a reaction. Picking during this phase is especially damaging, as it can worsen post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation on skin that is already prone to it.

Circadia SWICH is a medium-depth resurfacing peel and typically produces the most visible peeling of our menu — expect more pronounced flaking from days three through six, along with more initial redness. Heat avoidance and strict active-ingredient rest are especially important with this one.

GlyMed+ enzyme and chemical peels vary by formula but tend to be gentler, making them a good starting point for first-time peel clients or those with more sensitive skin. Downtime is usually minimal, though the same aftercare principles apply.

When you leave SkinArt after your peel, you will receive specific written aftercare instructions for the exact treatment you received. What follows is the general framework that applies across all of them.

What’s normal vs. what is not

Normal: Mild redness or warmth immediately after treatment, tightness or a slightly dry sensation in the first 24–48 hours, light to moderate flaking or peeling starting around day two to four, and increased sensitivity to sun and active products during the first week.

Not normal: Significant pain or burning that does not subside within a few hours, blistering, deep or widespread crusting, peeling that has not resolved after ten days, or any signs of infection (unusual warmth, swelling, pus). Reach out to your provider if you notice any of these.

Day 1–2: The settling phase

Immediately after your peel and through the first day or two, skin typically feels warmer and tighter than usual — similar to a mild sunburn sensation. Redness is common, and the skin may look slightly more flushed or have a subtle sheen. This is a normal inflammatory response; the peel is working.

Your only job during this phase is to be gentle. Cleanse with lukewarm (not hot) water and a mild, non-active cleanser. Skip all exfoliants, retinoids, vitamin C, AHAs, BHAs, and any other active ingredients — these will be reintroduced later, but not now. Apply a simple barrier-supporting moisturizer and a broad-spectrum SPF if you are going outside. Sleep on a clean pillowcase. Do not steam your face, take hot showers, do vigorous exercise, or spend time in a sauna — heat extends redness and can overstimulate already-sensitized skin.

Day 3–4: Flaking begins

Around day two to four — sometimes later with lighter peels — the skin begins to flake or peel visibly. This is the phase most people find difficult, because the temptation to pick or manually peel the skin is strong. It is exactly what you should not do.

Picking off skin that is not fully detached pulls living skin with it, which causes uneven texture, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and in some cases permanent scarring. If you know this is a challenge for you, keeping your nails short and your hands busy helps. Let the shedding happen on its own schedule. Keep skin well moisturized during this phase — hydrated skin sheds more comfortably — and be especially gentle when cleansing: pat, never rub, and skip washcloths or any form of physical exfoliation entirely.

Sunscreen is non-negotiable at this stage. Freshly treated skin is significantly more vulnerable to UV-induced pigmentation — the kind that can directly reverse the brightening effects the peel just created.

Day 5–7: Active peeling wraps up

For most superficial and medium-depth peels, visible peeling resolves within five to seven days. The skin beneath is smoother, brighter, and often more even-toned — this is the result beginning to reveal itself. Sensitivity typically decreases noticeably by the end of the first week.

Continue with gentle cleansing, moisturizer, and SPF. You can usually return to most normal activities at this point, though vigorous exercise, saunas, and steam rooms are best avoided until the skin has fully stabilized. Hydrating serums like hyaluronic acid are generally fine to reintroduce after day five, but hold off on vitamin C, retinol, AHAs, and BHAs until your esthetician clears you — typically once all visible peeling has resolved and the barrier feels intact again.

Week 2 and beyond: Results become visible

By the end of week two, the full result of a single peel is usually visible: smoother texture, more even tone, reduced dullness, and in many cases noticeable improvement in fine lines or early pigmentation. Skin often feels and looks noticeably refreshed.

For more significant concerns — deeper hyperpigmentation, acne scarring, pronounced texture changes — a single peel produces real improvement, but meaningful and lasting change typically comes from a series of treatments spaced three to four weeks apart. Each session builds on the last. Consistent sun protection between sessions is essential both to protect the work done and to prevent existing pigmentation from resetting.

What to put on your skin — and what to skip

Use: A gentle, fragrance-free cleanser; a ceramide-based or barrier-repairing moisturizer; a mineral SPF 30 or higher (physical sunscreen tends to be less irritating on post-peel skin than chemical filters); and a hydrating serum like hyaluronic acid once the initial sensitivity has passed.

Avoid: Retinol and all retinoids, vitamin C serums, AHAs (glycolic, lactic, mandelic), BHAs (salicylic acid), physical scrubs or exfoliating tools, clay masks, alcohol-based toners, face mists with fragrance or essential oils, and heavy liquid foundations during active peeling. All of these either accelerate cell turnover, compromise the barrier, or create friction on skin that needs to be left alone.

Many of our clients at SkinArt are already using GlyMed+ or Circadia products from our skincare shop. Both lines include gentle, barrier-appropriate formulas that work well during recovery — if you are unsure which of your current products are safe to continue using, ask at your appointment and we will walk through your routine with you.

A note on summer peels in Huntingdon Valley

Clients in the Huntingdon Valley and Philadelphia area often ask whether summer is a good time to get a peel. The honest answer: it depends on your lifestyle and how committed you are to sun protection during recovery.

The Philadelphia-area UV index regularly reaches 8–10 from June through August. If you are spending time outdoors — in the garden, at outdoor events, doing the school run — freshly peeled skin faces real risk of UV-induced hyperpigmentation that can undo the peel’s brightening effect. That does not mean we skip peels entirely in summer. BioRePeel, in particular, is designed for year-round use with appropriate candidates. But the peel choice, timing, and aftercare plan all factor in your summer schedule. We discuss this at consultation so there are no surprises.

The non-negotiables

Sunscreen, every single day. The most critical aftercare step, full stop. Unprotected sun exposure on post-peel skin can create hyperpigmentation that takes months to correct.

No picking or peeling. Let the skin shed on its own timeline. Manual interference creates more damage than the problem it seems to solve.

Keep it simple. A gentle cleanser and barrier-supporting moisturizer are all you need during recovery. A complex routine does more harm than good at this stage.

No actives until cleared. Your skin is already doing intensive resurfacing. Adding retinoids, acids, or vitamin C before the barrier has recovered disrupts the process and can compromise your results.

Avoid heat. Hot showers, vigorous workouts, saunas, and steam rooms prolong redness and irritation. Give your skin a full week before reintroducing intense heat.

Frequently asked questions

  • Is it normal for skin not to visibly peel after a chemical peel?

    Yes. Not all peels produce visible flaking — especially lighter superficial peels or BioRePeel, which is designed to resurface without visible shedding. Skin renewal is still happening at the cellular level even when there is no outward peeling. Your esthetician will tell you exactly what to expect based on the specific peel used.

  • How long should I avoid retinol after a chemical peel?

    At minimum seven to ten days after a superficial peel, and longer after medium-depth treatments. Retinoids accelerate cell turnover, which is already happening aggressively post-peel — layering more acceleration causes irritation, barrier compromise, and can produce uneven results. Wait until all visible peeling has resolved and your esthetician clears you before reintroducing.

  • Can I wear makeup after a chemical peel?

    Avoid makeup for the first 24 to 48 hours. After that, a mineral-based powder or foundation is the gentlest option during active peeling. Avoid heavy liquid foundations that tug at lifting skin. Always cleanse thoroughly at night, and be especially gentle removing makeup during the peeling phase.

  • When will I see results?

    Most clients notice smoother texture and improved clarity within one to two weeks as the fresh skin layer fully reveals itself. For deeper concerns like pigmentation, acne scarring, or pronounced texture changes, results build progressively over a series of treatments. Consistent SPF use between sessions is essential to protect and extend results.

  • What should I do if my skin peels unevenly?

    Uneven peeling is common and normal — the face rarely sheds uniformly. Do not pick or pull skin that is not fully detached. Keep skin moisturized, avoid all exfoliants, and let the process complete on its own. If you are concerned about how things look, contact your esthetician directly rather than intervening yourself.

  • Can I get a peel if I am currently using retinol?

    You will need to pause retinol use before your peel — typically five to seven days before the appointment, depending on the strength of your retinoid and the peel planned. Using retinol leading up to a peel increases skin sensitivity and raises the risk of over-exfoliation. Always disclose your current skincare routine at your consultation so we can advise on the right prep timeline for you.

  • Should I do a single peel or a series?

    A single peel produces real, visible improvement — especially for texture, brightness, and early pigmentation. A series of three to six peels, spaced three to four weeks apart, is where you see more meaningful change for concerns like deeper hyperpigmentation, acne scarring, or significant texture irregularity. We discuss this at your first appointment so you know what to realistically expect from one session versus a longer plan.
