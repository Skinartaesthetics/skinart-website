Post Care

What to Expect After a Chemical Peel — Day by Day

Post Care May 2026 By Dina Telesh

A professional chemical peel is one of the most effective ways to improve skin texture, tone, clarity, and pigmentation — but the results you see depend as much on what happens in the days after as on the peel itself. How you care for your skin during the recovery window determines how smoothly it heals and how well the final results land.

Here is a realistic, day-by-day guide to what to expect and what to do.

What’s normal vs. what is not

Before walking through the timeline, it helps to know the difference between a normal healing response and a reason to call your provider.

Normal: Mild redness or warmth immediately after treatment, tightness or a slightly dry sensation in the first 24–48 hours, light to moderate flaking or peeling starting around day two to four, and increased sensitivity to sun and active products during the first week.

Not normal: Significant pain or burning that does not subside within a few hours, blistering, deep or widespread crusting, peeling that has not resolved after ten days, or any signs of infection (unusual warmth, swelling, pus). Contact your provider if you notice any of these.

Day 1–2: The settling phase

Immediately after a peel and through the first day or two, skin typically feels warmer and tighter than usual — similar to a mild sunburn. Redness is common, and the skin may look slightly more flushed or have a subtle sheen. This is normal inflammatory response; the peel is working.

Your only job during this phase is to be gentle. Cleanse with lukewarm water and a mild, non-active cleanser. Skip all exfoliants, retinoids, vitamin C, and any other active ingredients — these will be reintroduced later, but not now. Apply a simple barrier-supporting moisturizer and SPF if you are going outside. Do not put makeup over skin that is still actively warm or sensitive unless absolutely necessary, and keep product application light-handed.

Day 3–4: Flaking begins

Around day two to four, depending on the depth of your peel, the skin begins to flake or peel visibly. This is the phase most people find difficult, because the temptation to pick or peel the skin manually is strong — and it is exactly what you should not do.

Picking off skin that is not fully detached can pull living skin with it, leading to uneven texture, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, or scarring. Let the shedding happen naturally. Keep skin moisturized so it feels more comfortable as it sheds, and be especially gentle when cleansing — pat rather than rub, and skip washcloths or scrubs entirely.

Sunscreen is non-negotiable during this phase. Freshly treated skin is significantly more vulnerable to UV-induced pigmentation, which can reverse the brightening effects the peel just created.

Day 5–7: Active peeling wraps up

For most superficial and medium-depth peels, the visible peeling phase resolves within five to seven days. The skin beneath looks smoother, brighter, and often more even-toned — this is the result beginning to reveal itself. Sensitivity typically decreases noticeably by the end of the first week.

Continue with gentle cleansing, moisturizer, and SPF. You can usually return to most normal activities at this point, though sweaty exercise, saunas, and steam rooms are best avoided until the skin has fully stabilized. Do not reintroduce active ingredients until your esthetician clears you — typically after all visible peeling has resolved and the barrier feels intact.

Week 2 and beyond: Results become visible

By the end of week two, the full result of a single peel is usually visible: smoother texture, more even tone, reduced dullness, and in some cases noticeable improvement in fine lines or early pigmentation. Skin often feels and looks noticeably refreshed.

For more significant concerns — deeper hyperpigmentation, acne scarring, pronounced texture changes — a single peel produces improvement, but meaningful change typically comes from a series of treatments spaced two to four weeks apart. Each session builds on the last. Consistent sun protection between sessions is essential to protect the work done and prevent pigmentation from resetting.

The non-negotiables of peel aftercare

Sunscreen, every single day. This is the most critical aftercare step. Freshly treated skin is highly susceptible to UV damage, and unprotected sun exposure can cause hyperpigmentation that takes months to address.

No picking or peeling. Let the skin shed on its own schedule. Manual interference causes more problems than it solves.

Keep it simple. A gentle cleanser and a barrier-supporting moisturizer are all you need during recovery. A complex routine backfires at this stage.

No exfoliants, retinoids, or strong actives until cleared. Your skin is already doing intensive resurfacing — adding more acceleration disrupts that process and can compromise the result.

Avoid heat. Hot showers, vigorous exercise, saunas, and steam rooms can prolong redness and irritation during the first week.

Frequently asked questions

  • Is it normal for skin not to visibly peel after a chemical peel?

    Yes. Not all peels produce visible flaking, especially lighter superficial peels or peels like BioRePeel, which is designed to resurface without visible shedding. Skin renewal is still happening at the cellular level even when there is no outward peeling. Your esthetician will tell you what to expect based on the specific peel used.

  • How long should I avoid retinol after a chemical peel?

    Most estheticians recommend waiting at least seven to ten days before reintroducing retinol after a superficial peel, and longer after deeper treatments. Retinoids accelerate cell turnover, which is already happening aggressively post-peel — adding more can cause irritation, redness, and barrier compromise. Wait until all visible flaking has resolved and your esthetician clears you.

  • Can I wear makeup after a chemical peel?

    It is best to avoid makeup for the first 24 to 48 hours to let the skin settle. After that, a mineral-based foundation or powder tends to be the gentlest option during active peeling. Avoid heavy liquid foundations that can tug at lifting skin, and always cleanse thoroughly at the end of the day.

  • When will I see results from a chemical peel?

    Most clients notice smoother texture and improved clarity within one to two weeks as the fresh skin layer fully reveals itself. For deeper concerns like pigmentation, acne scarring, or significant texture changes, results build gradually over a series of treatments. Consistent SPF is essential to protect and extend those results.

  • What should I do if my skin peels unevenly?

    Uneven peeling is common and normal. Do not pick or peel skin manually — pulling off skin that is not ready to detach can cause hyperpigmentation, scarring, or uneven texture. Keep skin moisturized, avoid exfoliants, and let the process complete on its own timeline. If you are concerned, reach out to your esthetician directly.
