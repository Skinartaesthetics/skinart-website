Microneedling

Microneedling 101: What It Treats & What to Expect

Microneedling August 2026 By Dina Telesh

Microneedling shows up on a lot of "best treatments for texture and scarring" lists, but the name alone tends to raise more questions than it answers. What is microneedling, exactly? Does it hurt? And how many sessions does it actually take to see a difference? Here is a clear, no-hype look at what this treatment does, what a session feels like, and what to expect afterward.

What Is Microneedling, Exactly?

Microneedling — also called collagen induction therapy — is a minimally invasive treatment that uses a professional device with fine, sterile needles to create controlled micro-channels in the surface of the skin. Those micro-channels trigger the skin's natural wound-healing response, prompting it to produce fresh collagen and elastin to repair the treated tissue.

At SkinArt Aesthetics, microneedling is performed with the SkinPen, a professional-grade collagen induction device, with needle depth adjusted between 0.25mm and 1.00mm depending on the area being treated and your skin's specific needs. The results are not immediate or dramatic — they build gradually as new collagen forms beneath the surface over the weeks that follow.

What Does Microneedling Treat?

Microneedling supports the skin's natural renewal process and may help improve the appearance of:

  • Atrophic acne scars — shallow, pitted, or rolling scars respond best
  • Fine lines and superficial wrinkles
  • Enlarged or congested pores
  • Rough or uneven skin texture
  • Mild skin laxity or loss of firmness
  • Uneven skin tone or post-inflammatory discoloration

It's worth noting that microneedling for acne scars works best on shallow, rolling scarring rather than deep ice-pick scars, and it is not a treatment for active acne or open breakouts — that needs to be addressed first. Results vary by individual, and a licensed provider should set realistic expectations at your consultation rather than promise a specific outcome.

What Happens During a Session?

Before Treatment

A consultation is required before your first microneedling session so your provider can review your skin history, current medications, and any conditions that might affect candidacy. Arrive with clean skin, free of heavy products or makeup.

Numbing

A topical numbing cream is applied and left on for about 20 to 30 minutes, which significantly reduces discomfort. Most clients describe the treatment itself as a mild prickling or vibrating sensation rather than pain.

The Treatment

The device is guided across the treatment area in a controlled pattern, with depth and pressure adjusted based on the area and your skin's tolerance. A full session, numbing included, typically runs about 75 minutes.

After Treatment

Your skin is finished with calming, barrier-supportive products selected for your individual needs, and your provider walks you through aftercare before you leave.

What Does Recovery Look Like?

Microneedling creates intentional, controlled micro-injuries, so a short recovery period is part of the process:

  • Immediately after: skin appears red and flushed, similar to a moderate sunburn; some pinpoint bleeding is normal
  • Day 1–2: redness and sensitivity peak — avoid active skincare, makeup where possible, and direct sun
  • Day 3–5: skin may feel tight or slightly rough as surface repair begins, with some mild flaking
  • Day 5–10: most visible redness resolves and skin starts to look smoother
  • Longer term: collagen remodeling continues for weeks to months, which is where the lasting improvement in texture and scarring actually happens

For the first 24–48 hours, skip retinoids, AHAs, BHAs, vitamin C serums, saunas, and excessive sweating, and wear broad-spectrum SPF 30+ daily for at least a week afterward. If you have an event coming up, plan your last session at least 10–14 days ahead of it.

How Many Microneedling Sessions Will You Need?

This is one of the most common questions we hear, and the honest answer is: it depends. For most clients, a series of 3 to 6 sessions spaced 4 to 6 weeks apart produces the most meaningful improvement in texture and scarring, with maintenance sessions once or twice a year after that. Mild concerns, like early fine lines or minor texture issues, tend to need fewer sessions; deeper acne scarring or more pronounced laxity usually benefits from a longer series. Your provider will map out a realistic plan at your consultation rather than guess.

Is Microneedling Right for You?

Microneedling may not be appropriate if you have active acne or open skin infections in the treatment area, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are currently using or have recently used isotretinoin, have a history of keloid scarring, are on blood-thinning medication, or have active eczema, psoriasis, or rosacea in the area to be treated. This isn't a complete list — if you're managing a medical skin condition, it's worth discussing with a dermatologist before booking, since estheticians are skin care professionals rather than medical providers. If you're under a dermatologist's care already, your provider can coordinate with their guidance.

For most healthy adults looking to improve texture, scarring, or early signs of aging, though, microneedling is one of the more well-studied, in-treatment-room options available. It's one of several advanced skin treatments we offer at SkinArt Aesthetics, and it pairs well with a broader plan that includes homecare and, for some clients, complementary treatments like a peel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is microneedling different from a chemical peel?

Microneedling creates micro-channels mechanically to stimulate collagen, while a peel uses an acid solution to exfoliate the surface layers of skin. Both can improve tone and texture, but they work through different mechanisms, and some clients benefit from including both in a treatment plan over time. If you're weighing the two, our chemical peel aftercare guide is a useful next read.

Will microneedling help my acne scars?

It may help improve the appearance of shallow, pitted, or rolling acne scars by stimulating collagen in the scarred tissue over a series of sessions. Deep ice-pick scarring typically responds less predictably. Expect gradual improvement, not a single-visit transformation.

Is microneedling safe for darker skin tones?

When performed by a trained provider at appropriate settings, yes — though deeper skin tones carry a somewhat higher risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation with any skin-disrupting treatment. This is part of what gets discussed at your consultation.

How many microneedling sessions will I need?

Most clients need 3 to 6 sessions spaced 4 to 6 weeks apart, depending on the depth of the concern being treated.

How soon can I wear makeup after microneedling?

Plan to wait at least 24 to 48 hours to minimize infection risk and allow initial healing.

Do I need a consultation before my first session?

Yes. A consultation lets your provider review your skin history and medications and confirm you're a good candidate before your first treatment — the same approach we use before recommending any customized facial or treatment plan.
