What to Expect at Your First Facial: A Complete Guide for New Clients

New Client Guide September 2026 By Dina Telesh

Booking your first facial can feel like a bigger decision than it needs to be. Will it hurt? What if your skin is a mess? What are you actually supposed to do once you're in the chair? If you've been putting off that first appointment because you're not sure what to expect at your first facial, this guide walks through the entire visit — from the moment you book to the days afterward — so you can walk in relaxed instead of guessing.

Why Every First Visit Starts With a Skin Consultation

Before any products touch your skin, a good esthetician wants to understand it. At SkinArt Aesthetics, every first appointment begins with a conversation: your skin goals, current concerns, allergies, medications, and your existing skincare routine all factor into the plan for that day. This isn't a formality — it's what separates a customized facial from a one-size-fits-all spa treatment, because oily, dry, sensitive, and acne-prone skin all need genuinely different approaches.

If you're not sure which facial fits your skin, this step is exactly where that gets sorted out. You can also book a standalone Skin Consultation first if you'd rather talk through your skin before committing to a full treatment — the consultation fee is applied toward a same-day facial if you decide to go ahead.

How to Prepare for Your First Facial Appointment

A little preparation goes a long way toward a smooth first visit:

  • Arrive with a clean or minimally made-up face if you can, though a full cleanse is always part of the treatment either way.
  • Avoid retinoids, strong exfoliating acids, or at-home peels for two to three days beforehand, especially if your skin tends to be reactive.
  • Note any new medications, skincare products, or recent sun exposure so you can mention them during your consultation.
  • Skip intense sun exposure or tanning in the day or two before your appointment.
  • Come a few minutes early for first-time paperwork and your consultation conversation.

None of this needs to be perfect — the point of the consultation is to work with whatever state your skin is actually in that day.

What Happens During Your First Facial, Step by Step

Every facial is customized, so exact steps vary, but a first appointment generally follows this shape:

  • Consultation and skin assessment: a closer look at your skin type and current concerns, informing the products and techniques used that day.
  • Double cleanse: removing makeup, sunscreen, and surface buildup to create a clean base.
  • Exfoliation: enzyme-based, chemical, or mechanical exfoliation, chosen based on how your skin tolerates it.
  • Extractions, if needed: gentle manual extraction of congestion, performed conservatively on a first visit.
  • Mask and targeted treatment: a mask or serum selected for hydration, calming, brightening, or clarifying, depending on your skin's needs.
  • Facial massage: to support circulation and help you actually relax during the appointment.
  • Moisturizer and SPF: sealing everything in and protecting your skin before you head back out.

The room is private, one-on-one, and unhurried — you're welcome to ask questions at any point along the way.

The Essential Reset: A Good Starting Point for First-Time Clients

If you're not sure which treatment to book first, The Essential Reset is designed with exactly this in mind. It's a foundational, 60-minute facial that deeply cleanses, gently exfoliates, and restores balance without the more intensive add-ons of our other signature facials — a genuinely good way to let your esthetician get a read on your skin before recommending anything more targeted for future visits. From there, treatments like microneedling or a chemical peel can be layered in once your skin and your esthetician both have a clearer picture of what you need.

What You Might Feel During and After

Most clients describe a facial as relaxing rather than uncomfortable. You may notice mild pressure during extractions or a brief tingling sensation from certain masks, but nothing should feel painful — speak up at any point if something feels like too much, and your esthetician will adjust. Afterward, it's normal for skin to look slightly flushed or pink for a few hours, especially if extractions were part of your treatment. This typically settles down by evening.

Aftercare: What to Do (and Avoid) in the Days After

What you do after your first facial matters almost as much as the treatment itself:

  • Keep makeup minimal for about 24 hours to avoid friction and irritation on freshly treated skin.
  • Skip strong actives — retinoids, acids, and physical scrubs — for a few days afterward.
  • Wear SPF daily, even if you already do; freshly treated skin is more sensitive to sun exposure.
  • Avoid saunas, steam rooms, or intense workouts for about 24 hours.
  • Follow any product recommendations your esthetician gives you specific to what was used during your treatment.

Making the Most of Your Ongoing Facial Routine

One facial can leave your skin looking noticeably brighter and smoother, but the real value of professional skincare comes from consistency. Most clients do well with a facial every four to six weeks, timed with the skin's natural renewal cycle — our guide on when to book your next facial breaks this down further. And because no two visits look exactly alike here, it's worth understanding why a customized facial beats a trendy one-size-fits-all treatment as you start building a routine that actually keeps up with what your skin needs. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a facial hurt?

A professional facial should not be painful. You may feel mild pressure during extractions or a brief tingling from certain masks or serums, but a good esthetician checks in throughout and adjusts pressure and product choice to your comfort level, especially at a first visit.

What should I wear to my first facial appointment?

Wear whatever is comfortable. You'll be reclined with your hair pulled back and a cover over your clothing, so there's no special outfit needed. Arriving with minimal makeup, if possible, simply saves a few minutes of cleansing at the start.

How long does a first facial appointment take?

Plan for 60 to 90 minutes for your first visit. That includes a skin consultation and assessment in addition to the treatment itself, which typically runs 60 to 75 minutes depending on the facial selected.

Can I wear makeup after my first facial?

We recommend keeping makeup minimal for about 24 hours to reduce friction and irritation on freshly treated skin. If you need coverage the same day, a light mineral SPF is usually fine.

What if I have sensitive or acne-prone skin?

Let your esthetician know before treatment begins. Every facial at SkinArt Aesthetics is built around your skin's current condition, so products, pressure, and technique are adjusted for sensitivity or active breakouts rather than following a fixed protocol. For persistent or medical-grade acne, a dermatologist remains the appropriate primary provider.

Do I need to book a consultation separately from my facial?

Not necessarily. A skin assessment happens at the start of every facial appointment, and the consultation fee is applied toward a same-day treatment if you'd like to book a standalone consultation first. Either path works well for a first visit.
