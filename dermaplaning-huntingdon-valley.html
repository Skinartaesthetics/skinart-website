What Is Dermaplaning?

Dermaplaning is a manual exfoliation technique performed by a licensed professional using a sterile, single-use surgical-grade blade. The blade is held at a precise 45-degree angle and moved across the skin in short, controlled strokes to gently scrape away the very top layer of the stratum corneum (dead skin cells) along with the fine vellus hair — often called peach fuzz — that sits on the surface of the face.

The result is immediate: the surface texture smooths noticeably, the skin appears brighter and more luminous, and product penetration improves because there is no longer a layer of dead cells and hair creating a barrier.

Benefits of Dermaplaning

Professional dermaplaning delivers several immediate and cumulative benefits. Clients most commonly come to SkinArt for the instantly smoother skin texture and the visible brightening effect that comes from removing accumulated surface buildup. Additional benefits include improved absorption of serums and moisturizers applied during and after the treatment, a smoother base for makeup that eliminates the texture that peach fuzz can create, and the reduction of the dull, flat appearance that builds up between cell-turnover cycles.

Because the technique does not involve any chemicals or devices, it is a gentle option for skin that is too sensitive for chemical exfoliation or mechanical tools.

Does Hair Grow Back Thicker or Darker?

No. This is one of the most common misconceptions about dermaplaning. Vellus hair (peach fuzz) does not have a hair follicle that can be altered by surface shaving. The hair grows back at the same rate, the same color, and the same texture as before. The blunt tip left by the blade may feel slightly different during regrowth compared to the fine tapered tip of a natural hair, but the hair itself is unchanged.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dermaplaning?

Dermaplaning is well-suited for most skin types, including dry, dull, or rough-textured skin that lacks radiance. It is particularly beneficial for those who want to improve product absorption, prepare for an event or photoshoot, or simply refresh the skin between more intensive treatments.

Dermaplaning is not recommended for clients with active acne breakouts, open skin lesions, inflamed rosacea, or highly sensitized skin. The blade should not be used over active blemishes as it can spread bacteria and cause irritation. If you have current active breakouts, we’ll recommend an alternative approach at your consultation.

What to Expect During Your Dermaplaning Session

Dermaplaning at SkinArt is a calm, comfortable treatment. The appointment begins with a thorough double cleanse to remove all traces of product, oil, and SPF from the skin’s surface. The dermaplaning itself is performed on dry skin — no steam, no product — using a sterile surgical-grade blade that is single-use only.

The sensation is often described as light, feathery scraping across the skin — similar to the feeling of shaving but much more controlled and precise. Most clients find it relaxing. After the dermaplaning is complete, a customized mask and calming serum are applied to nourish the freshly treated skin before finishing with SPF.

How to Prepare

  • Come with clean skin and no makeup if possible
  • Avoid retinoids and active exfoliants for 3–5 days before your appointment
  • Let us know about any recent facials, peels, or in-office treatments — there should be adequate recovery time between procedures
  • Do not book a dermaplaning appointment if you have active breakouts

Aftercare

Dermaplaning has minimal downtime. The skin is immediately smoother and brighter but also more exposed after exfoliation, so the first 24–48 hours are when aftercare matters most:

  • Wear SPF 30+ the day of and every day following treatment
  • Avoid direct sun exposure and heat (saunas, hot yoga) for 24 hours
  • Skip active ingredients — retinoids, acids, vitamin C — for at least 48 hours
  • Do not apply heavy or pore-clogging makeup immediately after treatment
  • Keep your routine minimal and hydrating for the first few days

Full aftercare guidance is available on our Post Care page.

How Often Should You Dermaplane?

Dermaplaning is typically performed every 4–6 weeks, aligned with the skin’s natural cell-turnover cycle. More frequent treatments are not recommended as the skin needs time to rebuild its natural protective surface between sessions. Dermaplaning works well as a standalone monthly treatment or as a prep step layered into a broader treatment plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will dermaplaning make my hair grow back thicker?

No — this is a myth. Vellus hair (peach fuzz) cannot be altered by surface dermaplaning. The hair grows back at the same color, thickness, and rate. The only change is that the blunt-cut tip may feel slightly coarser during the first week of regrowth, but this is temporary and does not indicate any actual change in the hair.

Does dermaplaning hurt?

Dermaplaning is a non-painful treatment. Most clients describe the sensation as light, feathery scraping or a gentle gliding feeling across the skin. It is one of the more comfortable exfoliation options available professionally.

Can I get dermaplaning if I have acne?

Dermaplaning is not appropriate for skin with active breakouts. The blade can spread bacteria from active blemishes and cause additional irritation. If you have active acne, we will recommend a more suitable treatment at your consultation. Dermaplaning may be re-introduced once your skin is clear and stable.

How is professional dermaplaning different from at-home dermaplaning tools?

Professional dermaplaning uses a surgical-grade, sterile single-use blade handled by a trained esthetician with precise technique. At-home dermaplaning tools are designed to be smaller and safer for use without training, but they also do not exfoliate at the same depth or consistency as a professional treatment. The professional version removes significantly more dead skin and is integrated into a broader facial protocol with appropriate pre- and post-care.

Can I combine dermaplaning with a chemical peel?

Yes — this combination is very effective. Dermaplaning first removes the surface barrier of dead cells and vellus hair, allowing a chemical peel to penetrate more evenly and efficiently. The two treatments work well together for texture, pigmentation, and overall radiance improvement. We’ll discuss whether the combination is appropriate for your skin at your consultation.
