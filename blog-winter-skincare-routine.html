Your Winter Skincare Routine: How to Protect Skin from Cold Weather and Dry Indoor Heat

Seasonal Skincare October 2026 By Dina Telesh

Every year, right around the time the first cold snap arrives in the Philadelphia suburbs, the same messages start coming in: my skin feels tight, my moisturizer is not working anymore, and my face looks dull. If that sounds familiar, you are not imagining it. Your winter skincare routine should look different from your summer one, because the conditions your skin is dealing with have changed. Here is how to adjust, simply and gently, so your skin stays comfortable until spring.

Why your skin changes in winter

Cold air holds far less moisture than warm air, and once you step indoors, forced-air heating dries things out even more. Add long, hot showers and wind on the walk to your car, and your skin loses water faster than it can replace it. Over time this can weaken the skin barrier, the protective outer layer that keeps hydration in and irritants out.

A compromised barrier often shows up as tightness, flaking, dullness, makeup that clings to dry patches, or skin that suddenly stings with products it used to tolerate. If you are not sure whether your skin is dry or simply dehydrated, our guide on dehydrated skin vs. dry skin explains the difference and why it matters for your routine.

Build your winter skincare routine in four steps

1. Switch to a gentler cleanser

Foaming or stripping cleansers can leave winter skin feeling squeaky, which is usually a sign that too much has been removed. Choose a creamy or lightly gel-based cleanser, use lukewarm water rather than hot, and pat your skin dry instead of rubbing.

2. Layer hydration before moisture

Hydration and moisture are not the same thing. Water-binding ingredients such as hyaluronic acid draw water into the skin, and a richer moisturizer helps seal it in. Apply a hydrating serum to slightly damp skin, then follow with a moisturizer that supports the barrier. If your skin is very dry, a slightly richer cream at night can make a noticeable difference.

3. Keep wearing sunscreen

It is easy to retire sunscreen when the days get short, but UV exposure does not take the winter off, and snow can reflect it back at your face. A daily broad-spectrum sunscreen protects the results of everything else in your routine.

4. Rethink your actives

Retinoids, exfoliating acids, and scrubs can be wonderful, but winter skin is often less tolerant of them. You may not need to stop, but you may need to use them less often, pair them with extra hydration, and listen to your skin. If you notice stinging, peeling, or lingering redness, pull back and focus on repair first.

Small habits that protect dry winter skin

Skincare products do only part of the job. A few everyday habits help your barrier hold on to moisture: shorten hot showers and moisturize within a few minutes of getting out, run a humidifier in the bedroom if your home is very dry, and do not forget the areas that take the brunt of the weather, like lips, hands, and the skin around your nose. Drinking enough water and eating well supports your skin from the inside, though it works best alongside, not instead of, a good routine.

When to see a dermatologist

Everyday winter dryness can usually be managed with gentle care, but some symptoms deserve medical attention. If your skin is cracking or bleeding, intensely itchy, red and inflamed, or not improving after a couple of weeks of consistent care, please see a dermatologist. Conditions such as eczema and contact dermatitis can look like simple dryness, and they are best evaluated by a medical professional.

Winter skincare routine: the takeaway

A good winter skincare routine does not need to be complicated. Cleanse gently, hydrate and seal in moisture, wear sunscreen daily, and ease up on strong actives when your skin feels fragile. Keep the routine consistent, and give your skin professional support when it needs it.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is the best winter skincare routine for dry skin?

    Keep it simple: a gentle cleanser, a hydrating serum, a moisturizer that supports your skin barrier, and sunscreen every morning. At night, repeat the cleanse, hydrate, and moisturize, and use exfoliants or retinoids only as often as your skin tolerates.

  • Why does my skin get so dry in winter?

    Cold outdoor air holds less moisture, and indoor heating dries the air even further. Together they can pull water from the skin's surface and weaken the barrier, which is why skin may feel tight, look dull, or flake more easily.

  • Should I still wear sunscreen in winter?

    Yes. UV exposure continues year-round, including on cloudy Pennsylvania days, and snow can reflect it back toward your face. Daily sunscreen is one of the simplest ways to protect your skin all year.

  • Can I still use retinol and exfoliating acids in winter?

    Many people can, but winter skin is often more reactive. Consider using them less often, pairing them with extra moisture, and pausing if you notice stinging, peeling, or persistent redness. Your esthetician can help you adjust your routine.

  • How often should I get a facial in winter?

    Many clients benefit from a facial about every four to six weeks, and winter is a good time to focus on hydration and barrier support. Your esthetician will suggest a rhythm based on your skin.

  • When should I see a dermatologist for dry skin?

    If your skin is cracking, bleeding, intensely itchy, or not improving with gentle care, see a dermatologist. Persistent dryness can sometimes be related to conditions like eczema, which are medical concerns rather than cosmetic ones.
